How to use it

  1. Only one arrow is shown — the very next move, nothing further.
  2. Start at ① and drag your finger to the arrow's tip.
  3. Reach the tip and the next arrow appears. The letter is never shown in full beforehand.
  4. A new number means: lift the finger and start a new stroke.
  5. Finish them all and it says the letter out loud, then praises you.
  6. Pick your pencil colour from the dots above the buttons.
  7. The arrows then clear away and it asks if you want to learn it again.
For teachers / parents

The arrows show correct stroke order and direction, which is what handwriting instruction actually teaches — a letter drawn with the right strokes in the wrong order becomes hard to write at speed later.

Only the next single move is revealed, so the child builds the letter stroke by stroke instead of copying a finished picture — they have to recall and produce each movement rather than trace an outline. Once they stop writing the arrows clear away, leaving their own attempt visible, and repeating is offered rather than imposed. Nothing is scored or timed.

How this works (data-science notes)

MediaPipe Hands finds 21 landmarks on your hand each frame. Geometric features (which fingers are extended) feed a rule-based classifier — a fast, transparent baseline. Collect samples above, then train a real KNN / Random-Forest model with train_gesture_classifier.py and compare the two. Testing on a separate recording session is what gives an honest accuracy number.
